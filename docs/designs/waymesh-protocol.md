## Executive Summary & Problem Statement

Centralized mobility and logistics monopolies (Uber, Lyft, DoorDash, Instacart) extract 30% to 45% platform commissions, dictate opaque matching algorithms, and trap transportation capacity inside closed walled gardens. Simultaneously, millions of everyday journeys take place with surplus empty capacity—vehicles travel with empty seats and vacant trunks, while local communities face exorbitant delivery fees and prohibitive single-rider fares. 

Existing open-source and decentralized alternatives have historically stumbled on two fatal bottlenecks:
1. **App fatigue & the two-sided liquidity cliff:** Dedicated apps struggle to convince users and drivers to download, register, and maintain another standalone client in a single city.
2. **Blockchain/Web3 latency & gas costs:** Putting high-frequency GPS tracking and spatial matching on public ledgers creates crippling transaction fees and latency, while naive chat bots (like LibreTaxi) lack verifiable trust, automated routing, and safety guarantees.

**Waymesh** solves this by defining an **open, federated protocol for peer-to-peer mobility and cargo logistics**, built with a **headless, agent-native architecture**. Instead of forcing users onto a monolithic app store download, Waymesh nodes expose first-class conversational connectors to **ChatGPT, Claude (via Model Context Protocol - MCP), and OpenWebUI**, while powering self-hosted community nodes written in high-performance **Rust with PostgreSQL + PostGIS**. 

Furthermore, Waymesh separates spatial coordination from optimization algorithms via a pluggable **Solver Abstraction Layer**, guaranteeing that whether matching is performed by greedy heuristics today or polynomial-time quantum algorithms in a hypothetical **P = NP** future, the underlying protocol, data models, and identity primitives remain completely invariant.

## Demand Evidence

- **Waymate Traction & The App Trap:** The founder previously launched *Waymate* (an earlier peer carpooling and ridesharing app), successfully acquiring **150+ user signups** organically. However, real-world operation revealed that friction inherent to standalone apps (onboarding fatigue, permission prompts, app updates) suppressed everyday engagement.
- **Extreme Single-Passenger Price Penalties:** In student and suburban transit corridors, taking an auto-rickshaw or taxi to a railway station or airport frequently costs *more than the long-distance train ticket itself*. For instance, a 25 km solo ride to a central junction typically costs ₹300–₹450 (e.g. ₹360 solo), whereas the subsidized intercity rail ticket is ₹120. Splitting a ₹300–₹360 fare across 3 passengers reduces the individual cost to ₹100–₹120/seat, directly matching or beating the train ticket itself (and a 4-passenger carpool reduces a ₹400 fare to ₹100/seat).
- **Unserved Piggyback Logistics:** Parents and local merchants frequently scramble to send urgent items (care packages, home food, documents, forgotten keys) to students or relatives at campus or transit stations. Commercial couriers require 24–48 hours and charge premium rates, whereas hundreds of peers travel the exact same railway corridor daily with empty cargo capacity.

## Status Quo & The Workaround Tax

Users currently resort to fragmented, unsafe, manual workarounds:
- **Chaotic WhatsApp & Telegram Groups:** Ride-sharing and drop-off requests are posted into unindexed chat groups. Messages get buried in seconds; drivers and riders face unpredictable no-shows; pricing is negotiated awkwardly via DMs; and there is zero verification of driver identity, parcel contents, or physical delivery.
- **Monopoly Surcharge:** When chat coordination fails, users fall back to commercial ride-hailing apps, surrendering 30–40% of the fare to platform intermediaries, enduring arbitrary surge pricing, and suffering high driver cancellation rates on suburban routes.
- **Wasted Capacity:** Daily highway commuters burn fuel with empty passenger seats and empty trunks, unable to monetize their existing travel because joining Uber/Ola requires commercial licensing, full-time commitment, and intrusive tracking.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

### Primary Persona (The Anchor Traveler & Passenger)
- **The Student / Campus Commuter:** Travels regularly along a defined 20–60 km corridor between a university campus/township and a major railway junction or airport. Highly price-sensitive, digitally native, and eager to split travel costs.
- **The Weekend Commuter / Parent:** Travels from home to the transit hub and is willing to carry a small, sealed care package or peer parcel for a student in exchange for offsetting fuel/toll expenses.

### The Beachhead Wedge
- **The Transit Hub Corridor (Campus ↔ Railway Station / Airport):**
  Instead of attempting a blanket city-wide on-demand taxi launch, Waymesh concentrates initial deployment on a high-density, fixed-corridor transit route. Every train departure creates synchronized clusters of passengers and parcel delivery requests moving in the exact same direction within tight time windows.

## Core Architectural Principles

1. **Open-Source and Community-Owned:** Released under Apache-2.0. No proprietary protocol extensions or vendor lock-in.
2. **Independent Self-Hostable Nodes:** Any student union, cooperative, housing society, or local fleet can spin up a sovereign Waymesh node in minutes via Docker Compose.
3. **Agent-First Accessibility:** Native integration with LLM interfaces (Claude MCP, ChatGPT Custom Actions, OpenWebUI) so users coordinate rides and deliveries via natural conversational language.
4. **No Mandatory Centralized Authority:** No central corporate servers, no global token requirement, and zero mandatory platform commission fees.
5. **P = NP Future-Proofed Solver Abstraction:** Decoupled matching and routing interfaces allowing pluggable solvers (Greedy, Integer Linear Programming, or future theoretical polynomial-time algorithms).
6. **Privacy by Default:** Exact origin/destination coordinates are protected by fuzzy spatial buffering until explicit mutual confirmation; journey corridors are matched without exposing private home addresses.
7. **Explicit Consent & Mutual Acceptance:** Both driver and passenger/sender must cryptographically or explicitly confirm terms before any contact information or exact pickup point is revealed.
8. **Verifiable Two-Stage Handovers:** Cryptographic time-bounded OTP tokens and digital signatures for pickup and delivery custody tracking.
9. **Resilient Local & Offline Operation:** Local nodes cache corridor state; cash and direct peer-to-peer settlement are first-class primitives.
10. **Low-Resource Footprint:** Written in memory-safe, high-throughput Rust with PostgreSQL + PostGIS, capable of running efficiently on a $5/month VPS or local mini-PC.

1. **Distribution via Chat/AI Connectors:** Dedicated mobility apps fail on two-sided install friction. Waymesh wins as a headless, sovereign protocol exposed directly to conversational AI assistants (ChatGPT Actions, Claude MCP, OpenWebUI) so travelers can publish and discover journeys through natural conversation.
2. **Transit Corridor Wedge:** Sustainable density is achieved by focusing on high-frequency transit hub corridors (e.g. campus ↔ railway station) where solo fares are disproportionately high and passengers can seamlessly carry peer parcels.
3. **Self-Hosted Nodes with Pluggable Solvers:** High-throughput spatial matching belongs on lightweight, self-hosted community nodes (Rust + PostGIS) equipped with a solver trait abstraction, peer-to-peer/cash settlement, and verifiable OTP custody proofs.

## Architectural Comparison & Approaches

### Approach A: Minimal Viable Monolith
- **Summary:** Single Rust binary (Axum + PostGIS) with embedded REST and basic MCP endpoints.
- **Verdict:** Ruled out. While fast to build, combining all logic in one monolithic crate limits community contributions, makes headless daemon embedding rigid, and prevents clean federation.

### Approach B: Ideal Distributed Mesh (libp2p + OSRM from Day 1)
- **Summary:** Full multi-crate workspace with libp2p GossipSub federation, distributed hash tables (DHT), and local OSRM road graph containers from Day 1.
- **Verdict:** Ruled out. Requiring gigabytes of RAM for OSRM graph files and complex p2p NAT traversal creates massive operational friction for small community operators and stalls initial deployment.

### Approach C: Agent-Native Modular Hybrid (Recommended & Chosen)
- **Summary:** Clean Rust workspace separating protocol core (`waymesh-core`), solver abstractions (`waymesh-solver`), HTTP/SSE engine (`waymesh-server`), and agent connectors (`waymesh-mcp`). Uses high-performance PostGIS spatial queries with an external routing abstraction (OSRM/Valhalla HTTP client or geodesic fallback). Exposes first-class Claude MCP tools and OpenAPI 3.1 endpoints for ChatGPT/OpenWebUI.
- **Rationale:** Achieves the perfect balance: immediate utility on transit corridors, turnkey single-command Docker Compose deployment, instant conversational AI support, and a mathematically rigorous solver abstraction ready for future optimization breakthroughs.

## Design for a P = NP Future: The Solver Abstraction

### Mathematical Problem Statement
Let:
- $J$ be the set of active journeys $j \in J$, where each journey has a spatial trajectory $T_j$, departure window $[t_j^{dep-min}, t_j^{dep-max}]$, passenger capacity $C_j^{pass}$, cargo volume $V_j^{cargo}$, cargo weight $W_j^{weight}$, and maximum allowed detour tolerance $\Delta_j^{max}$.
- $R$ be the set of passenger ride requests $r \in R$, with pickup $p_r$, dropoff $d_r$, earliest pickup $t_r^{earliest}$, and deadline $t_r^{latest}$.
- $P$ be the set of parcel dispatch requests $p \in P$, with pickup $p_p$, delivery destination $d_p$, dimensions $(l, w, h)$, weight $w_p$, and delivery deadline $t_p^{deadline}$.

In standard computational complexity, the joint **Multi-Commodity Dial-A-Ride Problem with Cargo Hitchhiking (MC-DARP-CH)** is strongly NP-hard. Current systems must rely on heuristics, spatial indexing filters, or mixed-integer programming (MILP) with localized branch-and-cut bounds.

In a hypothetical future where **P = NP** is proven and constructive polynomial-time algorithms exist, an optimal algorithm can instantaneously compute the global, network-wide Pareto frontier across thousands of federated nodes in polynomial time $O(n^k)$.

### Data Flow & Trait Separation

To guarantee zero coupling to specific database engines or complexity regimes:
1. **Repository Layer (PostGIS):** Performs spatial bounding-box indexing via `ST_DWithin` and `ST_Buffer` to retrieve candidate journeys within proximity and time constraints.
2. **Matching Engine Layer (`MatchingSolver` Trait):** Takes pre-filtered candidate journeys and demand items as pure in-memory data structures (`MatchProblem`), computes optimal route detours, and outputs `SolverSolution`.

```rust
use async_trait::async_trait;
use serde::{Deserialize, Serialize};
use chrono::{DateTime, Utc};
use uuid::Uuid;

#[derive(Debug, Clone, Serialize, Deserialize)]
pub struct GeoPoint {
    pub latitude: f64,
    pub longitude: f64,
}

#[derive(Debug, Clone, Serialize, Deserialize)]
pub struct CorridorGeometry {
    pub polyline: Vec<GeoPoint>,
    pub buffer_meters: f64,
}

#[derive(Debug, Clone, Serialize, Deserialize)]
pub struct JourneyCandidate {
    pub journey_id: Uuid,
    pub node_id: String,
    pub departure_time: DateTime<Utc>,
    pub available_seats: u32,
    pub available_cargo_volume_liters: f64,
    pub available_cargo_weight_kg: f64,
    pub corridor: CorridorGeometry,
    pub max_detour_minutes: u32,
}

#[derive(Debug, Clone, Serialize, Deserialize)]
pub struct DemandItem {
    pub demand_id: Uuid,
    pub demand_type: DemandType,
    pub pickup_location: GeoPoint,
    pub dropoff_location: GeoPoint,
    pub ready_time: DateTime<Utc>,
    pub deadline_time: DateTime<Utc>,
    pub seats_required: u32,
    pub cargo_volume_liters: f64,
    pub cargo_weight_kg: f64,
}

#[derive(Debug, Clone, Serialize, Deserialize)]
pub enum DemandType {
    PassengerRide,
    ParcelDelivery,
}

#[derive(Debug, Clone, Serialize, Deserialize)]
pub struct MatchProblem {
    pub journeys: Vec<JourneyCandidate>,
    pub demands: Vec<DemandItem>,
    pub optimization_objective: Objective,
}

#[derive(Debug, Clone, Serialize, Deserialize)]
pub enum Objective {
    MinimizeTotalDetour,
    MaximizeCapacityUtilization,
    MinimizeCostPerPassenger,
    ParetoOptimal,
}

#[derive(Debug, Clone, Serialize, Deserialize)]
pub struct MatchAssignment {
    pub demand_id: Uuid,
    pub journey_id: Uuid,
    pub estimated_pickup_time: DateTime<Utc>,
    pub estimated_dropoff_time: DateTime<Utc>,
    pub detour_minutes: f64,
    pub suggested_compensation_cents: u64,
    pub match_confidence_score: f64,
}

#[derive(Debug, Clone, Serialize, Deserialize)]
pub struct SolverSolution {
    pub assignments: Vec<MatchAssignment>,
    pub unassigned_demands: Vec<Uuid>,
    pub solver_identifier: String,
    pub computation_time_ms: u64,
}

#[async_trait]
pub trait MatchingSolver: Send + Sync {
    /// Return human-readable identifier and complexity class
    fn info(&self) -> SolverInfo;

    /// Solve the given matching problem in-memory
    async fn solve(&self, problem: MatchProblem) -> Result<SolverSolution, SolverError>;
}

pub struct SolverInfo {
    pub name: String,
    pub version: String,
    pub algorithm_type: AlgorithmType,
}

pub enum AlgorithmType {
    /// Fast greedy heuristic using vector trajectory alignment and detour bounding (O(N log M))
    SpatialHeuristic,
    /// Exact Branch-and-Bound / Mixed-Integer Linear Programming (Exponential worst-case)
    ExactMILP,
    /// Future polynomial-time optimal solver (P = NP theoretical adapter)
    PolyTimeOptimal,
}
```

### Swappable Implementations
1. **`SpatialCorridorHeuristic` (Default MVP):**
   - Evaluates candidate journeys retrieved by PostGIS pre-filtering. Ranks matches based on route angle alignment, detour minutes, and time distance. Computes in < 15ms.
2. **`OrToolsAdapter` (Near-term Optimization):**
   - Dispatches `MatchProblem` to Google OR-Tools vehicle routing solver for multi-stop dial-a-ride optimization.
3. **`P_Equals_NP_Adapter` (Theoretical Specification):**
   - Implements the exact same `MatchingSolver` trait. When a polynomial algorithm is discovered, it drops directly into the node without altering database schemas, API routes, or client connectors.

### Capacity Allocation & Concurrency Control
To prevent overselling seats or trunk space under concurrent booking requests:
- Booking acceptance executes within a serializable database transaction:
  ```sql
  UPDATE journeys 
  SET available_seats = available_seats - $requested_seats 
  WHERE id = $journey_id AND available_seats >= $requested_seats;
  ```
  If zero rows are updated, the booking attempt returns `409 Conflict (Seats no longer available)`.
- Parcel acceptance similarly decrements `cargo_weight_capacity_kg` and `cargo_volume_liters` atomically.
- Cancellation triggers an inverse atomic increment restoring the capacity.

## Authentication & Caller Identity Resolution

All mutating REST requests require identity verification via cryptographic headers:
- `X-Waymesh-Identity`: Caller's Ed25519 public key hex (64 characters).
- `X-Waymesh-Timestamp`: ISO-8601 timestamp (requests with drift > 300 seconds are rejected to prevent replay attacks).
- `X-Waymesh-Signature`: Ed25519 signature over UTF-8 string: `${HTTP_METHOD}:${REQUEST_PATH}:${TIMESTAMP}:${SHA256(REQUEST_BODY)}`.

For local conversational agents (e.g. Claude Desktop via MCP stdio or OpenWebUI tools running on the node machine), identity is resolved via the local daemon configuration (`WAYMESH_LOCAL_IDENTITY_KEY`), binding tool calls to the operator's registered sovereign key without exposing signing ceremonies to the end-user.

## Settlement & Sound Money Framework (Strict No-Crypto Policy)

Waymesh is intentionally decoupled from speculative financial tokens.

### 1. Strict Policy: No Cryptocurrencies or Web3 Tokens
Waymesh **does not** accept, issue, or integrate with cryptocurrencies, volatile tokens, gas fees, or blockchain ledgers. Cryptocurrencies introduce speculative churn, artificial financialization, and regulatory deadlocks that undermine real community transport.

### 2. Primary Settlement Mode: Private Direct Settlement Between Parties
- **Direct Peer Settlement:** All travel cost-shares, fuel splits, and delivery compensations are settled directly between parties privately without platform custody or commissions.
- **Mutual Agreement:** Travelers and couriers coordinate payments directly based on agreed rates, keeping value grounded between participants rather than through rent-seeking intermediaries.

### 3. Sovereign Direct Cash & Peer Digital Fiat
- **Physical Cash Handover:** Hand-to-hand fiat banknotes at the point of physical pickup or dropoff. Unmediated, offline, private, and zero transaction fee.
- **Direct National Peer Rails (UPI / SEPA / Pix / FedNow):** Direct peer-to-peer bank transfers (e.g. UPI in India) using native payment links or QR codes. Nodes never custody, touch, or take a commission from user funds.

## Security, Privacy & Threat Modeling (STRIDE)

| Threat Category | Potential Attack Vector | Waymesh Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **Spoofing** | Sybil nodes publishing fake journeys to harvest rider routes | Cryptographic Ed25519 identity keys; optional community vouching (e.g., student campus email verification token). |
| **Tampering** | Rogue actor altering parcel custody status or passenger count | Tamper-proof custody log with dual-sided OTP verification; hashes stored in PostgreSQL. |
| **Repudiation** | Carrier claims they never received a package; sender claims delivery failed | Two-stage handover: Carrier cannot advance state to `IN_TRANSIT` without Sender's Pickup OTP, and cannot claim compensation without Recipient's Delivery OTP. |
| **Information Disclosure** | Stalker harvesting user home coordinates from public journey streams | Spatial fuzzy buffering: exact coordinates are never broadcast to public search. Only a randomized 800m corridor polygon is exposed until mutual booking acceptance. |
| **Denial of Service** | Flooding node with bogus match queries or phantom bookings | Rate limiting per IP and identity key; dynamic stake/deposit or maximum active booking limits per identity. |
| **Elevation of Privilege** | Node operator stealing passenger payments | Zero custodial funds: Waymesh supports direct peer-to-peer settlement directly between parties privately via cash or direct UPI/bank transfer. No cryptocurrencies or speculative tokens are permitted, eliminating centralized financial honeypots. |
